99.9% Order Accuracy Isn't Luck — Here's How We Do It

February 27, 2026 4 min read By the MFS Team

The Number Most 3PLs Won't Put in Writing

Order accuracy is one of those metrics that sounds simple until you're the brand eating the cost of a wrong shipment. A mispicked item isn't just a refund — it's a support ticket, a frustrated customer, a potential chargeback, and a dent in your brand's reputation.

At MFS, we maintain a 99.9% order accuracy rate across every brand we fulfill for. That number holds during a slow Tuesday and during a 10,000-unit influencer drop. Here's exactly how.

It Starts With How the Warehouse Is Built

Most fulfillment errors happen at the pick stage. Someone grabs the wrong SKU, the wrong size, the wrong color. At scale, those mistakes compound fast.

Our warehouse is organized around a single principle: eliminate ambiguity. Every SKU has a dedicated bin location. Every bin is labeled with both a barcode and a human-readable description. There is no guessing, no "close enough," no relying on memory.

When a new brand onboards, we don't just dump inventory in an open area and figure it out later. We map product placement deliberately — grouping similar SKUs with enough physical separation to reduce the chance of a wrong pick.

Scan-to-Verify at Every Step

This is the one system that makes the biggest difference: every single item is scanned against the order before it gets packed.

Not spot-checked. Not reviewed at the end of a batch. Scanned, line by line, every time. If the scan doesn't match the order, the system flags it before that box gets sealed.

This matters because it moves the error catch from after the shipment — when it's already a customer problem — to before the shipment, when it's just a 10-second correction.

Packing Standards That Don't Vary by Shift

One of the quietest killers of accuracy in fulfillment is inconsistency between shifts and team members. When every person packs differently, errors hide in the gaps.

At MFS, packing standards are documented and trained — not just passed down verbally. For brands with specific requirements (custom inserts, branded tissue, specific folding for apparel), we build packing guides with photo references. Every team member follows the same process regardless of the time of day or order volume.

This also means that when a brand scales from 200 orders a day to 2,000, the process doesn't break down. It just runs faster.

Receiving Is Where Most 3PLs Lose Before They Start

Poor receiving is one of the most overlooked root causes of fulfillment errors. If inventory is miscounted, mislabeled, or incorrectly entered into the system on arrival, every downstream process is working with bad data.

When inventory arrives at MFS, it goes through a structured receiving process: quantity counts, SKU verification, condition inspection, and system entry before anything hits a shelf. If there's a discrepancy between what the brand says shipped and what we actually received, we flag it immediately — not three weeks later when a brand is wondering why they're overselling.

Clean receiving creates clean inventory data. Clean inventory data makes accurate fulfillment possible.

The Role of Real Accountability

Systems help, but accountability is what makes them stick.

At MFS, accuracy metrics are tracked at the individual and team level. When an error does occur — and at any volume, a small number will — we do a root cause analysis. Was it a labeling issue? A receiving discrepancy? A packing guide that wasn't clear enough? We fix the process, not just the symptom.

This is also why Drew stays close to operations instead of managing from a distance. When the founder is paying attention to the numbers that matter to your brand, the culture around those numbers is different.

What 99.9% Actually Means at Scale

To put the number in context: at 10,000 orders per month, a 99% accuracy rate means roughly 100 wrong shipments. At 99.9%, that drops to about 10.

That's not a rounding error — it's the difference between a manageable exception rate and a customer service problem that's eating your team's time and eroding your repeat purchase rate.

For DTC brands where customer experience is the product, fulfillment accuracy isn't an operational detail. It's a brand standard.

The Takeaway

A 99.9% order accuracy rate is the result of deliberate system design — not hustle, not hope. Smart warehouse layout, scan-to-verify picking, consistent packing standards, clean receiving, and real accountability all have to work together.

If your current 3PL can't tell you their accuracy rate, or won't put it in writing, that's worth paying attention to.
