99.9% Order Accuracy Isn't Luck — Here's How We Do It

June 18, 2026 4 min read By the MFS Team

The Number That Actually Matters

Most 3PLs talk about shipping speed. Fewer talk about accuracy — and the ones who do usually bury the real number in fine print.

Order accuracy is the metric that determines whether your customer gets what they paid for. A 98% accuracy rate sounds fine until you do the math: at 5,000 orders a month, that's 100 wrong orders. That's 100 refunds, 100 angry emails, 100 potential one-star reviews.

At MFS, we run at 99.9%. Here's exactly how.

It Starts Before the Order Is Picked

Most fulfillment errors don't happen at the packing station. They happen upstream — in how SKUs are stored, labeled, and organized.

Every product that comes into our facility goes through a structured receiving process. Items are verified against the inbound purchase order, scanned into inventory, and slotted into dedicated bin locations. No two SKUs share a bin. No exceptions.

This sounds basic. You'd be surprised how many warehouses skip it.

Barcode Scanning at Every Touchpoint

Human memory is not a fulfillment strategy.

Every item picked at MFS is scanned against the order before it ever reaches the packing station. If the barcode doesn't match the order, the system flags it immediately — the picker is alerted before a wrong item gets boxed. This single checkpoint eliminates the majority of pick errors before they become ship errors.

We scan again at pack. Two verification points per order, not one.

The Packing Station Is Designed to Reduce Mistakes

Pick-and-pack errors spike when stations are cluttered, rushed, or ambiguous. Our pack stations are clean, dedicated, and order-specific.

Packers work one order at a time. The packing slip shows a photo of the product — not just a SKU string. Visual confirmation matters. When you're moving 1,000+ orders a day, a photo check takes two seconds and prevents the kind of mistake that costs a customer relationship.

Inserts, packaging materials, and brand-specific requirements are documented in a brand playbook that lives at every station. Nothing is left to memory or verbal instruction.

Quality Control Isn't a Department — It's a Culture

We don't have a QC team that reviews a random 5% sample and calls it done. Quality control is baked into every step of the workflow.

That means the picker is accountable. The packer is accountable. And the shipping scan at the end of the line is a final confirmation that the right label is on the right box.

When an error does occur — and in any high-volume operation, they occasionally will — we do a root cause analysis. Not to assign blame, but to fix the process. Most errors trace back to one of three things: a labeling issue, a bin slotting problem, or a new SKU that wasn't properly onboarded. Each one gets a corrective action.

What 99.9% Actually Looks Like in Practice

At 10,000 orders per month, 99.9% accuracy means roughly 10 errors. That's not zero — but it's operationally excellent, and it's a number we track publicly with our brand partners.

Every MFS client has visibility into their error rate through our reporting. We don't hide mistakes. We surface them, own them, and fix them fast. That transparency is part of the model.

When an error is caught — whether by us or a customer — it gets resolved the same day. A replacement ships. The client doesn't have to chase us down for an answer.

Why This Matters More Than You Think

Fulfillment accuracy is a customer retention lever, not just an ops metric.

Research from the Baymard Institute and various post-purchase surveys consistently shows that a wrong or missing item is one of the top reasons customers don't repurchase from a brand. It doesn't matter how good your product is if the wrong thing shows up at the door.

For DTC brands investing in acquisition, a fulfillment error can erase the margin on that customer entirely. You paid to acquire them. You lose them to a pick error that cost $0.30 to prevent.

The Takeaway

Order accuracy at scale isn't magic and it isn't marketing copy. It's receiving protocol, barcode verification, station design, visual confirmation, and a culture where every person in the building owns the outcome.

If your current 3PL can't tell you their exact error rate — or won't — that's your answer.
