## The Number Most 3PLs Don't Want You to Calculate

Most 3PLs advertise an accuracy rate. What they don't do is help you run the math on what happens when they miss.

At 99% order accuracy — which sounds good on paper — a brand shipping 5,000 orders a month is generating 50 errors every 30 days. That's 50 customers who got the wrong item, a missing product, or a damaged shipment. Fifty opportunities to lose someone forever.

## What a Single Fulfillment Error Actually Costs

The surface-level cost is easy to see: a replacement unit, a return label, a refund. But that's rarely where it ends.

When a customer gets a wrong order, they don't just email your support team — they screenshot it, post it, and tell their friends. According to research from Esteban Kolsky, 13% of unhappy customers share their experience with 15 or more people. In a DTC world where word-of-mouth and UGC are currency, that's a cost that doesn't show up on any line item.

Break it down per error and it adds up fast:

- **Return processing**: $8–$15 per unit depending on your 3PL
- **Replacement shipping**: $6–$12 for ground, more for expedited
- **Lost product margin**: the unit that's gone, restocked, or discarded
- **Support labor**: 10–20 minutes of CS time per ticket
- **Customer LTV lost**: if a $120 AOV customer has a 3x LTV, that's $360 walking out the door

For a brand doing 5,000 orders a month at 99% accuracy, that's 600 errors a year. Conservatively priced at $30 each in hard costs alone — that's $18,000 annually. Before you factor in churn.

## Returns Don't Just Cost Money — They Cost Momentum

For scaling DTC brands, operational drag is a growth killer. Every hour your team spends processing return requests and issuing refunds is an hour not spent on marketing, product, or customer relationships.

Beyond the time sink, high return rates can trigger downstream problems: slower cash flow, inflated inventory counts, and in some cases, Shopify Payments holds if refund ratios spike. A fulfillment error isn't just a customer service problem. It's a finance problem.

## The Repeat Purchase Is Where You Actually Make Money

Most DTC brands don't profit on the first order. The margin is in the second, third, and fourth purchase. That's why LTV is the number that matters — and fulfillment errors destroy it.

A customer who receives the wrong order and has to fight for a replacement is not buying again. Full stop. You may win the refund battle and still lose the retention war.

The brands growing fastest right now understand that shipping is a brand touchpoint. The unboxing, the accuracy, the speed — that's part of the product experience. A 3PL that treats your orders like a commodity is handing your competitors their best acquisition tool.

## What 99.9% Accuracy Actually Looks Like in Practice

At MFS, we run at 99.9% order accuracy across all accounts. That's not a marketing number — it's a standard we hold ourselves to operationally.

How? Barcode scanning at every pick stage. Double-verification on high-SKU or subscription orders. A packing process designed to catch errors before they leave the building, not after they hit the customer.

When we do make a mistake — and no warehouse is perfect — we own it immediately and resolve it without making the brand or the customer wait in a ticket queue.

## The Real Question to Ask Your 3PL

Before you sign with any fulfillment partner, ask them: *What is your monthly error rate, and how do you track it?*

If they can't answer with a specific number, that's your answer. If they say "99%" like it's something to be proud of, run the math above and decide whether that rate fits your growth plan.

Fulfillment errors aren't a warehouse problem. They're a brand problem — and the brands that treat accuracy as a non-negotiable are the ones building customer relationships that last.
