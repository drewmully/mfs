## The Invoice That Changes Everything

Most brands don't realize their 3PL has a pricing problem until the invoice arrives.

It starts small. A "fuel surcharge" here. A "residential delivery fee" there. An "account maintenance fee" that nobody mentioned during onboarding. By the time you're doing real volume, these line items can quietly add 15-25% to your expected fulfillment costs.

That's not a rounding error. That's a margin problem.

## What Hidden Fees Actually Cost You

The obvious cost is financial. A brand doing $200K/month in revenue and expecting to pay $2.50 per order might actually be paying $3.10 or $3.40 once every surcharge is factored in. At scale, that's tens of thousands of dollars annually in unplanned spend.

But the harder cost is strategic. When you can't trust your cost-per-order number, you can't price your products accurately. You can't model unit economics. You can't make confident decisions about ad spend or inventory.

Unclear pricing doesn't just take money — it takes clarity away from your entire operation.

## Why 3PLs Do This (And What It Says About Them)

Some 3PLs build their pricing models around a low headline rate that wins the pitch, with the understanding that ancillary fees will make up the margin later. It's a short-term play that prioritizes new client acquisition over long-term retention.

The incentive structure tells you everything. A partner that needs to hide fees to stay profitable isn't operating efficiently — they're subsidizing their own model on your back.

A 3PL with nothing to hide quotes everything up front: pick and pack rates, storage fees, receiving fees, special project rates, and carrier markups. Every line item, explained, before you sign anything.

## What Transparent Pricing Actually Looks Like

Transparent fulfillment pricing isn't complicated. It's a clear rate card with no asterisks.

At MFS, every brand partner knows exactly what they're paying before their first order ships. Pick fees, pack fees, materials, storage — all of it is quoted plainly and doesn't change without a conversation. There are no fuel surcharges quietly baked into invoices, no "peak season" rate hikes that appear in December without warning.

When a brand can plug their MFS rate card directly into their financial model and trust the numbers, they can actually run their business.

## Pricing Transparency Is a Trust Signal

There's a downstream effect that goes beyond spreadsheets.

When a 3PL is upfront about pricing, it signals something about how they operate across the board. It means they're probably also upfront when an order gets delayed, when inventory counts are off, or when a carrier drops the ball. Transparency isn't a pricing policy — it's an operational culture.

Founders who've been burned by surprise fees once don't forget it. They pull volume, they switch partners, and they warn other founders in their networks. The DTC space is smaller than it looks.

Conversely, brands that trust their 3PL's pricing tend to stay. They grow into larger accounts. They refer other brands. That's how a fulfillment partnership actually works at its best.

## The Question to Ask Every 3PL Before You Sign

Before you commit to any fulfillment partner, ask them to walk you through every fee you could possibly encounter — not just pick and pack, but the edge cases.

Ask about: receiving fees per SKU or per pallet, return processing fees, special project labor rates, storage minimums, carrier surcharges and how they're passed through, and whether rates change during Q4.

If the answer is vague, or if they say "we handle that on a case-by-case basis," that's the answer. Ambiguity in pricing conversations almost always resolves against you.

## The Takeaway

Long-term fulfillment partnerships are built on operational performance and financial trust — and you can't have one without the other.

If your 3PL can't hand you a complete rate card and stand behind every number on it, you don't have a partner. You have a vendor who's betting you won't look closely at your invoices.
