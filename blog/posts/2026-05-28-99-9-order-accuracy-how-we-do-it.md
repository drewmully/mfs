## The Number That Actually Matters

Every 3PL has a pitch deck. Most of them say something about accuracy. What they rarely do is explain how that number is produced — or what happens when it slips.

At MFS, we track 99.9%+ order accuracy as an operational standard, not a marketing stat. That means for every 1,000 orders shipped, fewer than one leaves this building wrong. Here's what it actually takes to hold that line.

## It Starts Before the Pick

Most fulfillment errors don't happen during pick/pack. They happen before it — in how inventory is received, labeled, and slotted.

When a new SKU arrives at MFS, it goes through a receiving verification process before it touches a single shelf. Every unit is counted against the PO, inspected for damage, and assigned a barcode-linked bin location in our WMS (warehouse management system). If the count is off by even a few units, we flag it immediately rather than absorbing the discrepancy and discovering it months later during a customer complaint.

Garbage in, garbage out. Clean inventory data is the foundation everything else is built on.

## Two-Touch Verification on Every Order

Once an order hits the pick queue, our process requires two distinct verification points before a box is sealed.

The first happens at the pick station. Every item scanned against the order — wrong SKU, wrong quantity, the system flags it before the picker moves on. The second happens at pack, where the completed order is verified again before it's boxed and labeled. Two separate checkpoints, two separate opportunities to catch a mistake before it becomes a customer problem.

This isn't belt-and-suspenders overkill. It's the difference between a 99.9% accuracy rate and a 98% one — and at any meaningful order volume, that gap compounds fast.

## The Real Cost of a 1% Error Rate

A 1% error rate sounds small until you do the math. At 10,000 orders per month, that's 100 wrong shipments. Each one triggers a customer service interaction, a replacement unit, a return label, and a review that might not be kind.

Conservatively, a single fulfillment error costs a brand $15–$30 in direct recovery costs alone — before you factor in the customer who doesn't come back. At 100 errors per month, you're looking at $1,500–$3,000 in hard costs, plus the slower bleed of LTV erosion.

High accuracy isn't an operational nicety. It's a retention strategy.

## How We Handle Volume Spikes Without Losing Accuracy

Flash sales, influencer drops, subscription renewals — DTC brands don't ship in a steady, predictable drumbeat. Volume can double or triple overnight, and that's exactly when accuracy rates at most 3PLs start to slip.

Our approach is to pre-stage orders before peak windows hit. When we know a brand is launching a drop or running a promotion, we work with them ahead of time to stage inventory, pre-kit bundles where possible, and adjust staffing on the pick floor before the surge — not during it. Reacting to volume after it arrives is where errors get made.

Planning ahead isn't a luxury. At scale, it's the only way to protect accuracy when it matters most.

## Culture Is Part of the System

Processes matter. Technology matters. But the real reason our accuracy rate holds is that the people on the floor treat every order like it's going to a real person — because it is.

That sounds obvious, but it isn't universal. In large, anonymous warehouse operations, it's easy for a box to become just another box. At MFS, we're intentionally smaller and more accountable than the big-box 3PLs. Every picker knows their error rate. Every packer knows the brands they're working with. That accountability loop matters more than any piece of software.

## What This Means for Your Brand

If you're evaluating a 3PL, ask them one question: *What is your order accuracy rate, and how do you measure it?*

If they can't answer with specifics — a real percentage, a real methodology, a real process — that's the answer you need.

Accuracy at scale is earned through disciplined receiving, two-touch verification, proactive volume planning, and a team that's accountable to the number every single day. That's what 99.9% actually looks like from the inside.
