## Your 3PL's Address Is a Strategic Decision

Most DTC founders spend hours evaluating a 3PL's technology, pricing, and support model. Very few spend enough time looking at the map.

Where a fulfillment center sits geographically determines how fast your orders arrive and how much you pay to get them there. It's one of the highest-leverage decisions in your fulfillment setup — and it's often made last.

## How Carrier Zones Drive Your Shipping Bill

Carriers like UPS, FedEx, and USPS price shipments based on zones — the distance between the origin warehouse and the destination address. Zone 2 is cheap. Zone 8 is expensive. The further the package travels, the more you pay.

If your 3PL is located on the East Coast but 40% of your customers are in California, you're paying Zone 7 or Zone 8 rates on nearly half your orders. That adds up fast. A single-zone difference on a 10,000-order month can swing your shipping spend by $15,000 or more.

The math is simple. The awareness of it, surprisingly, is not.

## Why the Midwest Is a Quiet Advantage

A centrally located warehouse — think Ohio, Indiana, or Illinois — gives you access to the majority of the U.S. population within two to three days via standard ground shipping.

About 60% of the U.S. population lives within a two-day ground zone of the Midwest. For brands shipping via USPS Priority or UPS Ground, that means faster delivery without upgrading to expedited services. You're not paying for speed — you're getting it by default.

This is one of the core reasons MFS is headquartered in Ohio. It's not a coincidence. It's a calculated position.

## Shipping Speed Is a Retention Metric

Slow delivery isn't just an inconvenience — it's a reason customers don't come back.

According to a 2023 Shipbob consumer survey, 58% of shoppers say they won't reorder from a brand after a poor delivery experience. That's not a customer service problem. That's a logistics infrastructure problem.

When your warehouse is poorly positioned, you're asking customers to wait longer for no reason they can see or understand. They just know the package was slow. And they remember it.

## The Multi-Warehouse Trap

Some larger 3PLs pitch distributed inventory across multiple warehouses as the solution to zone optimization. In theory, it sounds smart — split your inventory on both coasts, reduce average zone distance.

In practice, for most DTC brands under $5M/month, it creates more problems than it solves. You're managing split inventory, dealing with stock imbalances, and paying two sets of storage fees. The operational complexity rarely justifies the marginal shipping savings at that scale.

For high-volume brands doing millions of units per month, distributed fulfillment makes sense. For everyone else, a single well-positioned warehouse usually wins on simplicity and total cost.

## What to Actually Ask a 3PL About Location

Before signing with any fulfillment partner, ask these questions:

- What percentage of your current clients' orders ship to Zone 5 or below from your facility?
- What is the average transit time for ground shipments to the top 10 U.S. metro areas?
- Do you have data on average zone distribution for brands in my category?

A good 3PL will have this data ready. A great one will walk you through it proactively.

## The Takeaway

Warehouse location isn't a footnote in your fulfillment decision — it's a line item in your P&L and a direct input into your customer experience.

Before you evaluate a 3PL's software integrations or packaging options, pull up a zone map and run the numbers on where your customers actually live. The right zip code for your warehouse might be worth more than any other operational optimization you're chasing.
