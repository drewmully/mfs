Why Transparent Fulfillment Pricing Builds Better Partnerships

July 1, 2026 4 min read By the MFS Team

The Invoice That Changes Everything

Most brands don't discover their 3PL's real pricing until the first invoice lands.

That's when the line items appear — fuel surcharges, account management fees, receiving fees per SKU, weekend handling premiums, returns processing add-ons. None of it was in the sales deck. All of it was buried in the contract.

This is how most 3PL relationships start to break down before they ever get a chance to work.

Hidden Fees Are More Common Than You Think

A 2023 survey of DTC operators found that over 60% had experienced unexpected fees from their fulfillment partner within the first 90 days of the relationship. That's not a rounding error — that's an industry problem.

The fees themselves are rarely massive in isolation. A $0.15 per-label surcharge, a $50 monthly reporting fee, a $1.00 oversize handling charge. But stack them across thousands of orders and watch what happens to your unit economics.

More damaging than the cost is the signal it sends: we were not fully honest with you upfront.

That signal is very hard to walk back.

What Transparent Pricing Actually Looks Like

Transparent fulfillment pricing isn't just a flat rate card. It's a clear, complete picture of every touchpoint where cost is generated — before you sign anything.

At MFS, every brand partner receives a full pricing breakdown before we onboard them. That means:

  • Receiving: Cost per unit or per hour, clearly defined
  • Storage: Monthly rate per bin, shelf, or pallet — no ambiguity
  • Pick and pack: Base rate per order plus per-item fees, stated plainly
  • Materials: What's included, what's not, and at what cost
  • Returns: Per-unit processing rate, no hidden labor fees
  • Special projects: Kitting, rebagging, relabeling — quoted before executed

Nothing appears on an invoice that wasn't already in the conversation. That's the standard.

Trust Is an Operational Asset

Founders talk about brand trust in terms of customer relationships. The same logic applies to your vendor relationships.

When you trust your 3PL's pricing, you can model your unit economics accurately. You can forecast. You can make confident decisions about pricing, promotions, and scaling. When you don't trust it, you're always waiting for the next surprise — and that cognitive overhead is expensive.

Long-term brand partnerships are built on predictability. Not perfection, but predictability. Knowing what fulfillment will cost in month one should feel the same as knowing what it will cost in month eighteen.

Why Big 3PLs Have a Pricing Problem

The large fulfillment networks are not inherently dishonest. But their pricing structures were designed for enterprise contracts negotiated by procurement teams — not for a 500-orders-per-day DTC brand run by a founder who's also handling customer service.

Complex fee tables serve a purpose at scale. At your scale, they mostly create confusion and erode margin in ways that are hard to track.

Boutique 3PLs — the ones with a smaller, more intentional client roster — have a different incentive structure. Our growth depends on your growth. That alignment makes transparency the obvious default, not the exception.

What to Ask Before You Sign With Any 3PL

Before committing to a fulfillment partner, get every one of these answered in writing:

  1. What triggers a surcharge or fee outside my base rate?
  2. How are fuel and carrier rate increases passed through?
  3. What is the returns processing fee, exactly?
  4. Are there minimum volume commitments, and what happens if I miss them?
  5. What does receiving cost if my shipment arrives outside a standard window?

A good 3PL will answer these without hesitation. A bad one will get vague.

The Long View

Brands that stay with a fulfillment partner for three or more years consistently cite one thing above shipping speed, above technology integrations, above warehouse location: they trust the people running their fulfillment.

Trust is built slowly and destroyed fast. Transparent pricing is one of the clearest signals a 3PL can send about how they intend to operate with you — not just in month one, but in month thirty-six.

If the pricing conversation feels uncomfortable before you've signed, it won't get easier after.
